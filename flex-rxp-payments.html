Flex redesign: payments

January 2025 to present • Flex

Flex helps customers split their rent payment into two installments over the course of a month instead of having to pay their largest (!) recurring cost all in one chunk. Users pay their first installment when rent is due, then Flex pays their rent directly their property using this first payment and a credit line. They repay Flex later in the month.

This project began as a revamp of our rent tab, led by my product partner Jessie and I. We did a lot of discovery & iteration that eventually fed into a larger app redesign.

For the redesign, I am on a team of designers working to redesign the consumer app. I lead all payments and payment-related things with Jessie.

Disclaimer: this is WIP so I can’t share too many screens! It will be mostly process outline.

The “rent status” problem

Users are confused about the status of their rent and payments, and more generally how to use Flex. Rent status confusion is our largest ticket driver; customers write in asking things like, “when will my rent be paid?”, “what do I need to do?”, “is something wrong?” Paying rent is core to our functionality, so we needed to do better.

Brainstorming

We broke apart the pieces of our product and started stitching together new ways to present the same information, pulling inspiration from as many places as possible. We looked at buy now pay later, direct competitors, and apps with cyclical or monthly actions.

Armed with a few concepts and a lot of questions, we set up a couple panels with a handful of relatively new customers. Which, if any, resonate with our existing customers? What info did they need and what were they thinking during their first month of splitting rent?

Our wireframe-y concepts
A whiteboard from a research session, pairing information together and sorting by relative importance

Ultimately, what we found is that our current rent tab didn’t answer the #1 question our customers had very well (when will my rent be paid?). First time users in particular had a lot of anxiety. Through designing, we also found that our existing app could not scale to multiple products.

The next concept

One of our big takeaways was that our existing rent tab wasn’t as far off as we thought it might be. The core pieces of information were there, but they were a bit muddled by the organization and density of information. We made a few key changes:

  • Rent status needed to be far more visible, so we pulled it into the new hero section.
  • Restructure the payments section to include all of the information we had in the original progress tracker
  • Say less when nothing is wrong so that when there *is* something wrong, it’s easy to find.
The new concept

Redesigning the app

However, the work on the rent tab along with some other initiatives were telling us we needed to do more than redesign the rent tab; ultimately, product leadership decided to launch a full-fledged app redesign.

Goals

  • Fix the rent status problem
  • Rethink the user experience with a consumer-first mindset
  • Create the infrastructure to scale to new verticals (from both a design systems perspective and an app IA & product strategy perspective)

Given that I was the designer with the most product context, I led the charge on redesigning all payment related actions. Our goal for payments was to align our product more closely with both classic fintech apps and rent portals.

As a design team, it means rebuilding a design system from the ground up and executing on a light visual brand refresh while actively designing with it.

A new payments ecosystem

We need to build a scalable payments UX in the same way engineering has a payments engine. Payments and their actions have a lot of moving pieces that all need to fit together, no matter the configuration.

What’s next

We’ve used this redesign opportunity to fine tune many flows in the user experience and identify areas that need deeper iteration and more cycles, research, and data. Our MVP will be delivered in May; stay tuned for outcomes!
